# Demo video screenplay

Target under 3:00. Left column is what gets recorded, right is the voiceover. About 330 spoken words.

## Prep, not recorded

- Julia logged in, in one window. Fresh signup page ready in another.
- Dry-run the agent prompts once so responses are warm. Record waits in full, cut them in the edit.
- Record inside ChatGPT's browser (or Chrome with WebMCP enabled) for scenes 4 to 6, chat panel visible beside the page so tool calls are on screen.
- Password stays masked.

## Scene 1, 0:00 to 0:18. Julia's September, month view. Slow scroll.

> All summer I watched the same model build my startup's backend, then fall apart planning my freshman year: misclicking around Stanford Axess, forgetting everything about me in seconds. The model was never the problem. The problem was context and browsing. So I built Acorn, the academic planning workspace for Stanford, one that you and your agent work on together.

## Scene 2, 0:18 to 0:45. Julia flythrough. Week view 4s, Academics 3s, Activities 3s, Scratchpad 3s, Activity panel 3s.

> This is Julia, a sample of a fully built workspace. Her September. The week her agent picked around ballet rehearsals and her Friday work-study shift. Her quarter plan and AP credit. Her clubs, with meetings that land on the calendar. Her scribbles next to the goal her agent structured. And the log of who did what, her or her agent.

## Scene 3, 0:45 to 1:05. From scratch. Signup, then onboarding: Name "Srivatsav Kannan", University Stanford, Entered 2026, Graduating 2030, Enter my workspace. Hold on the empty calendar.

> Now let's make mine, from scratch. A name and three timeline facts, and I'm in. It starts empty. Watch how fast that changes.

## Scene 4, 1:05 to 1:50. The braindump. Type to the agent:

"I'm Srivatsav, an incoming freshman. I'm into AI, healthcare, and startups. I want a research assistant spot by spring, and I lift in the mornings, so no classes before 10. Put all of that in my workspace, and mark CS 106B and MATH 51 as courses I'm interested in for autumn."

Flip once to Scratchpad and Academics while tools fire.

> Instead of filling out forms, I tell my agent who I am. The page registers twenty-two tools with the browser using WebMCP, so the agent isn't screen-reading or guessing where to click. It writes to the same workspace my clicks do. My goal, my no-mornings rule, the courses I'm curious about. It all lands while I watch.

## Scene 5, 1:50 to 2:10. By hand. Activities, Add your own: a club with a weekly Thursday 18:00 meeting. Show the calendar. Then ask:

"What does my Thursday look like now?"

> And it goes both ways. I add a club by hand, and it's on the calendar immediately. When I ask my agent about Thursday, it already knows, because we're reading and writing one shared context.

## Scene 6, 2:10 to 2:40. The schedule. Ask:

"Build my autumn schedule around all of that. Real sections only."

Let the tools fire, then hold on the filled week view.

> Then the real work. The catalog behind this is Stanford's real one, 15,618 courses, with sections that pair each lecture with its discussion. The agent walks the real combinations, keeps my mornings free, and puts the result on my calendar. The same model that built my backend just planned my quarter.

## Scene 7, 2:40 to 2:52. Landing page, cursor still.

> Acorn is live at acornplan.vercel.app. Next, it ships barebones and the agent builds the catalog for any school. Whatever the two of you put down, you come back and it is still there.
