# How Sri writes

Synthesized from the Stanford application essays, the IntroSems statements, the UPRound launch drafts, SRIVATSAV_MODEL_CONTEXT.md, and the way he talks in working sessions. This is the reference for anything published under his name.

## The shape of a piece

- Opens on something concrete: a scene, an object, a number, a line of dialogue. "Pedestrian." A newspaper report about 200 bags of biomedical waste. Everyone at YC Startup School having a take. Never an abstract thesis.
- Builds an arc: concrete experience, tension or question, a realization, action taken, and a closing image that echoes the opening.
- Ends on an earned image or a quiet punchline, never on a summary or a motivational line.

## Sentence mechanics

- Flowing, connected prose with varied lengths. A short sentence appears only when the moment earns it. No staccato fragment chains, no "This matters. A lot."
- States the point directly. Never "not X, but Y" stacks, never "it is not merely A; it is B."
- No em dashes. Commas, colons, parentheses.
- Parentheticals carry humor and texture (CORS errors alone used to eat my evenings).
- Questions appear sparingly and always get answered ("But how do I begin?").

## Texture

- Humor lives in specifics: degree kaapi at 1 a.m., labeling comfort food "do not touch" in four languages, squirrels raiding snacks. Nothing generic, nothing fabricated.
- Numbers do real work and are exact: 530+ facilities, 3,794 companies, $10,000, 663-day streak. Rounding is deliberate and grounded.
- Technical detail is welcome but always translated in passing (Schere for scissors, Schnitt for cut). Machinery stays out of marketing copy unless the audience is technical.
- Ambition shows through choices and consequences, never announced ("passionate," "resilient," "impactful" are banned).

## Banned

- Claudeslop tells: "delve," "leverage," "seamlessly," "transformative," "journey," "empower," "in today's fast-paced world," decorative emoji, canned three-part rhetoric, empty enthusiasm.
- Disclaimers and hedging in product copy. State the thing.
- Sentimentality. The strongest writing stays personal without going soft.
